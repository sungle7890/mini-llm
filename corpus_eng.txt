Meeting on the Red Land

Doyun loved the mornings on Mars. Morning here was nothing like morning on Earth. The sky was a pale brown, and the sun looked smaller and paler than the one he had known back home. Still, Doyun woke at the same hour every day and stood before the glass wall of the greenhouse. Beyond the glass stretched the endless red land. When the wind blew, fine dust drifted slowly through the air, hanging there for a long time before settling back onto the ground.

The Ares base was not large. There were twelve people in all, and each of them had a task. Doyun's task was the greenhouse. He grew the vegetables the people of the base would eat, and he tended the plants that kept the air clean. Doyun loved this work. There was no green on Mars. There was only the red, dry land. And so the small patch of green inside the greenhouse was precious to him beyond words.

Seoa was in charge of communications and geology. By day she studied the land of Mars, and by night she sent signals to Earth. Earth was far away. It took a long time for a signal to reach Earth, and a long time for an answer to return. Seoa was used to that slow conversation. There was no need to hurry. On Mars, everything moved slowly.

The first time the two of them spoke was one evening. That day the wind was unusually strong. A dust storm was approaching from far away, and the windows of the base had all turned brown and dim. Doyun stayed late, worried about the plants in the greenhouse. Seoa stayed late too, checking the communications. The two of them met in the narrow corridor.

"Still awake?" Seoa asked.
"I'm worried about the plants. When a storm comes, the greenhouse gets too cold," Doyun answered.
Seoa looked at him for a moment. "I stayed because I'm worried about the signal. When a storm comes, the link to Earth breaks."
They looked at each other and smiled a little. What they worried about was different, but the heart that kept them there late was the same.


The Green of the Greenhouse

From the next day on, Seoa often came by the greenhouse. When her communications work was done, she came and watched Doyun tend the plants. The greenhouse was the warmest place in the base. And it was the only place with any green. Seoa loved that green. To eyes that had seen only red land, the green was strange and welcome all at once.

"What plant is this?" Seoa asked, pointing at a small leaf.
"Lettuce. It grows well. It grows well even on Mars," Doyun answered.
"That's amazing. It grows even in a place this far away."
"Plants are stronger than you think. Give them water and light and they live anywhere. Mars is no different."

Seoa nodded. Doyun's eyes shone when he talked about plants. Seoa liked that. A person who truly cared for something was beautiful. Doyun looked the same way when Seoa talked about Earth. Seoa often spoke of the ocean. There was no ocean on Mars. Water was very rare, and most of it hid underground as ice.

"The ocean on Earth is truly wide," Seoa said. "You can't see the end of it. The waves roll in and roll out, and roll in again. I used to stand before it for a long time."
"I would like to see the ocean too," Doyun said. "Here I can only imagine it. I look at the red land and imagine that it is the sea."
Seoa laughed. "Then let's imagine it together. That the red land is the sea."
That evening, the two of them stood side by side at the greenhouse window and looked out at the red land. And they imagined that it was a wide ocean. The dust carried by the wind looked just like waves.


The Slow Conversation

A day on Mars was a little longer than a day on Earth. That small difference felt strange at first, but with time it became familiar. Doyun and Seoa grew a little closer every day. They did not hurry. On Mars everything moved slowly, and so did the hearts of the two of them.

Seoa told Doyun stories of Earth. Doyun told Seoa stories of the plants. Seoa knew how to watch the stars, and Doyun knew how to touch the soil. Each had what the other did not. And so each other's stories were always new.

"Why did you come to Mars?" Doyun asked one day.
Seoa thought for a moment. "I wanted to go far away. On Earth I always felt chased by something. Here it isn't like that. Time moves slowly. I like that."
"I'm the same," Doyun said. "On Earth, green was too common. So I never knew how precious it was. Here even a single small leaf is rare. I love that rareness."
Seoa looked at him. "We're alike."
"We are. We're alike."

At night, Seoa sent a signal to Earth. She wrote down what had happened that day, in a few short lines. That the lettuce in the greenhouse had grown, that the storm had passed, small things like that. The answer would not return for days. Still, Seoa sent a signal every day. Doyun sat beside her and watched her send it.


The Storm Comes

The storms of Mars were frightening. Once one began, it went on for days. The sky was covered entirely in brown, and the sun could not be seen. Dust covered everything. When the solar panels were covered in dust, the base ran short of power. When power ran short, it was hard to keep the greenhouse warm.

When the largest storm of that year came, the base's power fell dangerously low. To save power, the people said they should lower the greenhouse heating. Doyun objected. If they lowered the heating, the plants would freeze and die.

"People come before plants," someone said.
"I know. But without the plants there is no air either. The plants keep our air clean," Doyun said.
The people thought it over. Doyun was right too. But the power really was running out.

Then Seoa stepped forward. "I'll find a way. If we wipe the dust off the solar panels, a little power will come back. I'll go outside and clean them."
"You'd go out into the storm? It's dangerous," Doyun tried to stop her.
"There's no other way to protect the greenhouse," Seoa said. "It won't take long. I'll be right back."
Doyun wanted to stop her. But Seoa's eyes were already set. Doyun took her hand. "Then I'm going with you. I won't send you out alone."


The Night They Went Out Together

The two of them put on their suits and went outside. The storm was fierce. Dust blocked their sight so that they could not see even a few steps ahead. The wind pushed at their bodies. They tied themselves together with a rope, so as not to be torn apart.

"Don't let go of my hand," Seoa said.
"I won't let go," Doyun answered.

Slowly the two of them made their way to the solar panels. They wiped the dust away with their hands. No matter how much they wiped, the dust piled up again. Still they did not stop. It was to protect the green of the greenhouse. It was to protect the signal that went to Earth. It was to protect the things the two of them held dear.

How much time passed, no one could say. At last the panels grew a little cleaner. A little power returned to the base. The greenhouse heating came on again. The two of them came back inside. When they took off their suits, their whole bodies were soaked with sweat. Their hands were shaking. But the two of them were smiling.

"We did it," Seoa said.
"We did it because we were together," Doyun said.
That night, the lettuce in the greenhouse did not freeze. The signal to Earth did not break. The two of them sat at the greenhouse window and waited for the storm to pass. Outside was still brown, but inside was warm, and there was green.


After the Storm Passed

The storm ended after three days. The sky cleared again to a pale brown. The small sun rose once more. A faint light fell over the red land. The people of the base came out and looked at what the storm had left behind. Dust covered everything, but there was no great damage.

Doyun went to the greenhouse. The lettuce was safe. The leaves were still green. Doyun was relieved. Seoa came and stood beside him.
"It's safe," Seoa said.
"Thanks to you," Doyun said. "If it weren't for you, this green would have frozen and died."
"Because you tried to protect it. I only helped."
Doyun looked at her. And he said the words he had wanted to say for a long time. "Seoa, I love the time I spend with you. Mars does not feel strange to me, and that is because of you."
Seoa was quiet for a moment. Then she smiled softly. "Me too. I love the time I spend with you too. This red land is not lonely, and that is because of you."

The two of them held hands. Outside the window, the red land stretched on without end. When the wind blew, the dust flew like waves. They imagined that it was the sea. A wide, wide sea. It was a sea that belonged to the two of them alone.


The Answer from Earth

A few days later, a signal returned from Earth. It was the answer to the small stories Seoa had sent. The people of Earth loved Seoa's stories. The story of the lettuce growing on Mars, the story of the storm passing, the story of the two who wiped the dust from the solar panels. The people of Earth sent an answer to those stories. That they were doing well, that they were proud of them, that they should stay healthy.

Seoa read the answer aloud to Doyun. Doyun listened quietly. Earth was far. But though it was far, their hearts still reached. It was a slow conversation, yet even in that slowness there was tenderness.

"Don't you miss Earth?" Doyun asked.
"I do. Sometimes," Seoa answered. "I miss the ocean, I miss the green, I miss people. But there is something here to take the place of what I miss. Because here, there is you."
Doyun smiled. "It's the same for me. I miss Earth, but here there is you. And so it's all right."

Every day the two of them sent a signal to Earth. And every day they tended the plants in the greenhouse. It was small work. But those small tasks gathered into a day, and the days gathered into the time of the two of them.


The Seasons of Mars

Mars had seasons too. They were different from the seasons of Earth, but they were clearly seasons. In summer the ice melted a little, and in winter it froze again. The seasons turned slowly. A single year on Mars was close to two years on Earth. And so a single season was very long.

The two of them spent the seasons of Mars together. They spent the long summer together, and they spent the long winter together. In summer the plants of the greenhouse grew well. In winter they saved the heating and guarded the plants carefully. Each time the season changed, the two of them grew a little closer.

"How long have we been here?" Seoa asked one day.
"In Martian time, not long. In Earth time, quite a while," Doyun answered.
"Time is such a strange thing. Here it passes slowly, yet when I look back, we've already come this far."
"We came together. If I had been alone, I couldn't have come this far."
Seoa nodded. It was true. If she had been alone, Mars would have been far too lonely a place. But because they were two, Mars was bearable, and at times it was even beautiful.


Choosing to Stay

New word came from Earth. It was word that the next spacecraft would soon arrive at Mars. Whoever boarded that craft could return to Earth. The people of the base each had to choose. Whether to return to Earth, or to stay on Mars.

Doyun and Seoa talked for a long time. On Earth there was the ocean, green was common, and there were people they missed. On Mars there was only the red land. There were dust storms, there was precious water, and there was the small greenhouse. And there was each other.

"What do you want to do?" Seoa asked.
Doyun looked out for a long time at the red land beyond the window. "I want to stay. I cannot leave this greenhouse behind. I want to protect this green. And... I want to be with you."
Seoa smiled quietly. "I think the same. I've come to love this red land. I love spending the seasons here with you. I'll stay too."

The two of them decided to stay. The spacecraft took the others aboard and left for Earth. Doyun and Seoa remained at the base and watched it go. The craft vanished slowly toward the small sun. The two of them held hands and watched it for a long time.


The Sea of the Two of Them

After the craft was gone, the base grew a little quieter. But it was not lonely. It was because the two of them were together. Doyun still stood before the greenhouse window every morning. Seoa still sent a signal to Earth every night. The days of the two of them were the same as before. Only now, it was different, because each of them was by the other's side.

One evening, the two of them sat side by side at the greenhouse window. Beyond the glass, the red land stretched on without end. The sun was setting. The small sun sank slowly below the horizon. The sky was stained a pale red.

"Look over there," Seoa said, pointing at the red land. "Doesn't it look like the sea?"
"It looks like the sea," Doyun said. "Our sea."
The wind blew. The dust flew like waves. The two of them sat before it for a long time. The night of Mars came down slowly. The stars rose one by one. They were the same stars they had seen from Earth. Only a little clearer.

"Will we be able to live well?" Seoa asked.
"We will," Doyun answered. "Because we're together. Just as a plant grows anywhere with only water and light, we too can live anywhere as long as we are together. Mars is no different."
Seoa leaned her head on Doyun's shoulder. The two of them looked at the stars. Over the red land, the night grew deep. Inside the greenhouse it was warm, and there was green. Outside the window was a wide sea. It was a sea that belonged to the two of them alone. And over that sea, the stars shone.

The morning of Mars would come again. The pale brown sky would brighten, and the small sun would rise once more. The two of them would begin another day. They would tend the plants of the greenhouse, send a signal to Earth, and look out at the red land, imagining that it was the sea. There was no need to hurry. On Mars everything moved slowly, and the two of them still had many long seasons left to spend together.


The Water Underground

A season passed after that. The long summer of Mars came again. It was the season when the ice melted little by little. Every day Seoa studied the red land. Water was hidden beneath the ground of Mars. Most of it was ice, but in the deep places there might be flowing water too. Seoa wanted to find that water. With water, they could make the greenhouse larger. If the greenhouse grew larger, there would be more green.

"Today I'll survey beyond that hill," Seoa said one morning.
"Isn't it far?" Doyun asked.
"It's far, but it's worth going. I think there's water beneath the ground there. The signals are telling me so."
Doyun went with her. The two of them rode a small vehicle over the hill. The red land stretched on without end. Beyond the hill there was only more red land. But Seoa's eyes were reading the ground. Land that looked to Doyun like nothing but red soil looked to Seoa like a map of water.

Seoa drove a machine into the ground and waited a long time. The machine listened to the sounds of the deep earth. After a while, the machine sent a signal. Seoa's face lit up.
"It's here. There's water. It's deep, but it's clearly here."
Doyun looked at her. Seoa was smiling on the red land. That smile was more welcome than any green. The two of them had found water. Beneath the ground of Mars, they had found water that had hidden there for a long time.


A Larger Greenhouse

After they found the water, the two of them began to build a new greenhouse. It was a great task. It was too much for just the two of them. But they did not hurry. They built a little every day. One wall today, one window the next. And so, slowly, the greenhouse grew.

Doyun planted many plants in the new greenhouse. Not only lettuce. He planted tomatoes, he planted beans, and he planted small flowers too. Flowers on Mars. The very thought made Doyun's heart flutter. On the red land, inside the glass, small flowers would bloom.

"Will they bloom?" Seoa asked.
"They will. Because there is water and light," Doyun answered. "It will take time, but they will surely bloom."
Every day Seoa came by the new greenhouse. She watched the seeds sprout. Sprouts that at first looked like tiny dots became leaves after a few days. The leaves grew slowly. The plants of Mars grew more slowly than the plants of Earth. But they did not stop. If they did not stop, however slow, someday they would flower.

One morning, Doyun opened the greenhouse door. And he stopped. A single small flower had bloomed. It was a yellow flower. On the red land, inside the glass, a single yellow flower had bloomed. Doyun called for Seoa. Seoa came running. The two of them stood side by side before the flower and looked at it for a long time. It was the first flower to bloom on Mars.


The Photograph Sent to Earth

Seoa took a photograph of the flower. And she sent it to Earth, together with the news that a flower had bloomed on Mars. A yellow flower blooming on the red land. The people of Earth waited a long time for that photograph, and looked at it a long time.

A few days later an answer came from Earth. Many people had seen the flower, it said. One child had drawn the flower in a picture. One person had wept at the sight of it. A single small flower that had bloomed on Mars had reached countless people on Earth.

"The flower we planted has gone so far away," Seoa said.
"A single small flower," Doyun said. "Even a small thing travels far."
"Maybe it travels far because it is small. People's hearts are moved by small things."
The two of them talked for a long time that night. From Mars to Earth, from Earth to Mars. It was a slow conversation, but there was always tenderness in it. Though far, their hearts still reached. A single flower had proven it.


New People

The next spacecraft arrived at Mars. This time it brought people. They were people who would build a new colony. The Ares base was no longer a base of twelve. It became twenty, and it became thirty. On the red land, the people grew in number.

The newcomers were astonished at Doyun's greenhouse. So much green on Mars. Flowers blooming on Mars. Doyun taught the new people how to grow plants. That with only water and light, plants live anywhere. That Mars is no different. The new people learned Doyun's words. And each of them began to build a small greenhouse.

Seoa taught the new people how to read the land. Where there was water, where there was ice. The red land said nothing, but if you knew how to read it, it told you many things. The new people learned Seoa's words. And each of them began to survey the land.

The base grew larger and larger. The greenhouses increased, the green increased, and the people increased. On the red land, a small village came to be. When Doyun and Seoa had first arrived, Mars had been an empty land. Now people lived on Mars. It was becoming a land where green grew, where flowers bloomed, and where the laughter of children could be heard.


A Promise on Mars

One evening, Doyun and Seoa stood again in the corridor where they had first met. Now the corridor was wider, because there were more people. But to the two of them that corridor was still special. It was where they had first spoken.

"Do you remember?" Doyun asked. "We first met here. On the night the storm came."
"I remember," Seoa laughed. "You said you were worried about the plants, and I said I was worried about the signal."
"Back then I didn't know. That you would become someone so precious to me."
Seoa looked at him. Doyun took her hand. And he spoke the words he had long prepared.
"Seoa, I had already made up my mind when I chose to stay on Mars. I want to spend the seasons that remain with you. On this red land, beside this green, I want to grow old with you."

Seoa was quiet for a long while. Outside the window, the red land stretched on without end. The small sun was sinking toward the horizon. At last Seoa smiled softly.
"I feel the same. I have come to love this red land. I love imagining this land is the sea, beside you. I want to spend the seasons that remain with you."
The two of them held hands. On Mars they made a promise. It was not a splendid promise. But it was a promise as firm as the red land, and as lasting as the green.


A Small Wedding

The people of the base held a small wedding for the two of them. It was the first wedding on Mars. The place was Doyun's greenhouse. It was the warmest place, and the place with the most green. The people gathered in the greenhouse. They decorated it with the yellow flowers Doyun had grown. On the red land, inside the glass, among the green and the flowers, the two of them stood side by side.

There was no grand ceremony. The two of them simply held hands and looked at each other. And they made a short promise.
"I will spend the seasons together," Doyun said. "The long summers and the long winters, I will spend together."
"I will protect this land together," Seoa said. "The green, the water, and each other, I will protect together."
The people applauded. The greenhouse filled with laughter. Outside the window, the red land stretched wide. When the wind blew, the dust flew like waves. The two of them imagined that it was the sea. Now that sea was no longer theirs alone. It was the sea of everyone who lived on Mars.

Seoa sent a photograph of that day to Earth. The first wedding held on Mars. The green and the yellow flowers inside the greenhouse. The people of Earth looked at the photograph for a long time. And they sent an answer of congratulations. It was a slow conversation, but the joy reached them clearly.


The Time That Flowed On

Time passed. The seasons of Mars turned many times. The long summers came and went, and the long winters came and went. The two of them spent all those seasons together. White hairs grew in Doyun's hair. Lines grew at the corners of Seoa's eyes. But the way the two of them looked at each other was the same as at the start.

The base was now a large village. There were dozens of greenhouses. Green covered one side of the red land. Children ran between the greenhouses. Those children had been born on Mars. They were children who knew the red land as home. To those children Doyun taught how to grow plants, and Seoa taught how to read the land.

"Do you remember when we first came?" Seoa asked.
"I remember," Doyun answered. "Back then it was only the two of us. Mars was an empty land."
"Now so many people live here. So much green grows here."
"We started from something small. One head of lettuce, one flower. And it grew this large."
Seoa took Doyun's hand. Their hands were old, but warm. Outside the window, the red land still stretched on. And on one side of that red land, there was now green. It was the green the two of them had made.


Again, the Sea of the Two of Them

One evening, Doyun and Seoa sat again at the window of the old greenhouse. It was the same window where they had first looked out at the red land together. Now there was green beyond the window too. But past it, the red land still stretched on without end.

The sun was setting. The small sun sank slowly below the horizon. The sky was stained a pale red. The wind blew. The dust flew like waves.
"Look over there," Seoa said, pointing at the red land. "It still looks like the sea."
"It's still our sea," Doyun said.
The two of them sat for a long time. The night of Mars came down slowly. The stars rose one by one. They were the same stars they had seen from Earth. Only a little clearer.

"We lived well, the two of us," Seoa said.
"We lived well because we were together," Doyun answered. "Just as a plant grows anywhere with only water and light, we lived here because we were together. Mars was no different."
Seoa leaned her head on Doyun's shoulder. The two of them looked at the stars. Over the red land, the night grew deep. Inside the greenhouse it was warm, and there was green. Outside the window was a wide sea. It was the sea of the two of them. And over that sea, the old stars shone on, unchanging.

The morning of Mars would come again. The pale brown sky would brighten, and the small sun would rise. New people would begin another day. They would tend the plants of the greenhouse, send a signal to Earth, and look out at the red land, imagining that it was the sea, just as the two of them once had. There was no need to hurry. On Mars everything moved slowly, and now green grew upon the red land, and the people would live on beside that green, together, for a long, long time.
